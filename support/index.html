New to RSS?

Almost every blog and news site publishes a feed, a simple list of its newest posts. The Reading Room checks the feeds you choose and shows you what’s new, in order, with no algorithm in between. Inside the app, New to RSS? at the bottom of the sidebar (or Help › The Reading Room Guide on the Mac) walks you through it and suggests a few good people to follow.

Getting started

Add a feed

Press ⌘N, then paste a website address. The app finds its feed. Or type a few words, like pharmacy news, to follow that topic.

Pick writers by topic

New to the app, or looking for more people to read? Open the Guide and choose Pick writers by topic (it also shows up on its own the first time you open the app with no feeds). Pick a few topics, like The small web or Apple and the Mac, and follow the writers in them in one step. These are real people who write often and link to each other, so you’ll soon see them answering one another in Conversations. The list comes from readingroomapp.com; nothing about you is sent.

Follow a YouTube channel

Copy the channel’s address from YouTube, like youtube.com/@channelname, and paste it into + Add a feed. A playlist link works too and follows just that playlist. New videos show up with your other posts and play right in the app. YouTube’s feeds list only a channel’s newest 15 videos, so older ones won’t come in. You can also paste a link to any single video, and the app follows its channel. If YouTube won’t give a channel’s feed, the app reads the channel’s Videos page instead.

Make folders

Click the folder button next to Feeds, then drag feeds onto a folder. To put a folder inside another, like Blogroll › Charlotte NC, choose Inside when you make it, or drag one folder onto another; drag it onto All to bring it back to the top. Folders go two levels deep, and inner folders are listed first.

Coming from another reader?

Export your subscriptions as an OPML file there, then choose Feeds › Import OPML…. Folders come along.

Reading

Click a story to read it in the app. View original opens the full web page in the app’s own window.

Select text in an article and choose Highlight or Add note. Find them all under Annotated.

Search and finding blogs

Search, under Explore, looks everywhere at once: In what you follow (every post the app has kept), Blogs about your words (whole blogs, each with a post of theirs that matched and Follow), On personal sites (single pages, with Show more), and What people are saying on Bluesky and Mastodon if you’ve connected one. Typing in Search stories above a list only filters that list; its Search everywhere line takes the words to Search.

Discover’s search shows Blogs about first too. A person’s page in Conversations ends with Sites like this one: sites linked from the same places. New to the app? The first screen has Or find blogs about something else; tap the ones you want to add them to the writers you’ll follow. Blogs and personal sites are found with Marginalia Search, a search engine for the small, independent web.

In the Flickr tab, Search has Everyone and Your photos. Flickr only lets apps like this one search your own photos, so for everyone’s the app offers Flickr’s own search in its web window.

Conversations

Conversations turns the blogs you read into a social feed. Its tabs are Neighborhood, You and Search, plus Following once you connect Mastodon or Bluesky.

Neighborhood

The posts of the people you read from the last two weeks, newest first, with their faces. No account needed. “Since you were here” at the top sums up what’s new; fold it to one line with –. The faces under it are the people who posted in the last three days; a green ring around a face means something new from them you haven’t read yet, and it goes away once you’ve read it. Tap a face to see just their posts. When writers answer each other on their blogs, the replies gather under the original post as a thread. Faces, threads and links fill in as your feeds refresh, so give it a minute the first time. Following fewer than five people? It suggests a few to start with, and Writers your writers link to suggests blogs that the people you read keep linking to (× stops suggesting one).

Filters, groups and topics

The buttons under “Since you were here” narrow the Neighborhood: Conversations, Notes, Photos, Long reads, One from each (just the newest from each person) and Topics. On someone’s page, Groups puts them in a group of your own, which then shows as a button too. Tap a topic on a post to see everyone’s posts on it, or choose Mute this topic.

Close friends

On someone’s page, choose Close friend. Their new posts sit at the top of the Neighborhood under a star, and you get an alert when they post (switch it off on their page).

The small labels on posts

Tap one (on a Mac, point at it) and it says what it means. You is your own post; the star is a close friend; Reads you means you’re on their blogroll; Links to you means the post links to your site; Back after means their first post in months. The hearts and speech bubbles under a post are likes and replies other sites sent it; tap to see them. Liked by shows which people you read liked it on their own sites.

A page for each person

Tap a face or a name, or choose Their page on a feed: a line about who they are, their posts, who they talk with, emails from them, the blogs they link to and their blogroll, with Follow all. Unfollow, at the end of the row of buttons, asks first, then removes all of that person’s feeds and takes them off Close friends.

Like and reply

Like without a site connected keeps the like just for you. With your WordPress site connected (Settings › Connections), the first like asks to make a Likes page on your site; after that each like is added there and the writer’s site is told, if it accepts Webmentions. Like again to take it back. Reply opens a box in the post: post your reply on your own site, or email the writer. An unfinished reply waits as a draft. Type @ to mention someone you read, or select words in a post and choose Quote in a reply. Repost puts the post on your site as a short note; a repost of a video is marked as a repost, and its Bluesky copy plays the video. Tap Reposted ✓ to see your repost, or Repost again if you removed it. Tap “12 comments” to read a post’s comments in the app.

The Reply box tells you whether the writer’s site shows replies from other blogs. If it does, your reply can appear under their post. If not, it stays on your site, and the box offers their comments section when they have one.

Your page

The card at the top of You opens your own page: your posts, your blogroll and who links to you. If it asks which site is yours, pick it from the list, or add it.

Following and You

Following shows what people share on Mastodon or Bluesky once you connect an account; Bring your people over offers the blogs of the people you follow there. You gathers replies to you there, comments on your own site, blogs linking to you and emails from people you read; the buttons above it show just one kind. Your replies lists the replies you posted from your site, and you’re told when they answer. Alerts for replies, comments and mentions can be switched on there.

Search

When something big happens, type it into Search. The left column shows what people are saying right now on Bluesky and Mastodon (connect an account in Settings › Connections); the right column shows posts from the last week in the blogs you read. Recent searches stay as buttons, and Check again looks again.

Mark all as read

Conversations in the sidebar shows how many new posts and replies are waiting. Mark all as read is at the top of the Neighborhood.

Videos

The Videos tab

Videos, under People in the sidebar, gathers the YouTube channels and video feeds you follow, so they don’t crowd Today. Don’t want it? Turn it off in Settings › Appearance › Show in the sidebar, and videos go back into Today.

Channels to start with

While you follow only a few channels, the top of Videos suggests some to start with. Tap Follow and their new videos show up in Videos.

Up next

On a Mac or iPad, when no video is open, the right side of Videos shows Up next: your newest unwatched video, big and ready to play, with four more below it. Tap one to watch.

Channels your people share

At the top of Videos and Discover: YouTube channels the writers you read keep sharing in their posts, with who shared them. Tap a picture to watch, or Follow. Under a video in a post there’s a Follow button for its channel too. The list fills in over a few days as your feeds refresh.

Keep it playing

Leave a story while its video or episode plays, and it keeps playing in a small player in the corner. Tap its title to go back to the story, or × to stop it. Video files have Picture in picture, and on the Mac Open in its own window puts the video in a small window that stays on top.

Save for offline

Under a podcast episode or a video file, choose Save for offline. Saved ones play without a connection and show a Saved tag; Remove deletes one. Finished ones are removed after a week, and Settings › Saved for offline shows the space they use. YouTube and Vimeo videos can’t be saved; the YouTube app’s own Download is the way for those.

Comment on YouTube

YouTube, Vimeo, Rumble, Odysee, Dailymotion and TikTok don’t take replies from blogs. Write your reply, then tap Comment on YouTube (or the video’s site): your words are copied and the video opens in its app or your browser, ready for you to paste them under it.

Writing

Writing, under Read in the sidebar, is where you write for your own WordPress site: short Notes, full Posts and Weeknotes, with your ideas, your drafts and your blog’s numbers alongside. Connect your site with Connect your site on the Writing page; the app uses an Application Password you make in WordPress. Nothing goes on your site until you press Post or save a draft. Posting to your site is part of The Reading Room Plus. Don’t want it? Turn it off in Settings › Appearance › Show in the sidebar.

Write something

Pick Note, Post or Weeknotes, then write on a ruled notebook page. A post can have a title, a category and a featured image; a note can have a photo. Leave a blank line between paragraphs; a line starting with ## is a heading and one starting with > is a quote. The word count and reading time show by the buttons. Post now publishes it, Save as a draft on your site keeps it for later, and Also share on Bluesky or Mastodon posts a copy that links home. What you type is kept on your device until you post it.

Preview and Focus

Preview shows the post the way readers will see it before it’s on your site. Focus hides everything but the page; press Done, or Esc on a Mac, to come back.

A blank page

Tap Writing in the sidebar any time for a blank page. Anything you’d written is saved as a draft on your site first, and the app says so.

Ideas and drafts

Ideas keeps things you might write about, synced through iCloud; Write it starts a post from one. Drafts on your site lists your WordPress drafts: Keep writing opens one right here, Preview shows it, and Delete moves it to your site’s trash, with Undo. A draft can become a Note, Post or Weeknotes by tapping those buttons. Drafts made in WordPress’s block editor open in WordPress instead.

Folders and the calendar

Open the arrow next to Writing in the sidebar for folders: Ideas, Drafts, Posts, Notes, Weeknotes and your site’s own categories. The writing calendar marks the days you posted; tap a day to see what went on your site that day.

This week

This week walks you through your Weeknotes in three steps. The step you’re on has the green button, and each step gets a check when it’s done.

  1. Pick what you read (optional). Tap Pick posts, tick your favorites from what you read, saved or highlighted, and add a line about any of them. Your picks are kept for the week as you tick them. Skip this step and the posts you read go in instead.
  2. Write. Tap Write Weeknotes. It opens a page with what you posted since Monday under What I made, your picks under What I read, and room under What happened. Add a few words.
  3. Publish. Press Publish on the page, or Save as a draft to finish later. Keep writing always brings this week’s draft back, so there’s only ever one.

You can pick before or after you start writing; either way your picks end up under What I read. Want What I read as a post of its own? Tap Pick posts, then Write it as a post.

Your blog

Writing shows how your site is doing: visitors over the last 28 days and the posts read most, the RSS readers that follow you, by reader app, and your search traffic from Google. Your site needs the free plugin The Reading Room stats; without it, Writing shows where to get it.

The Reading Room Plus

Reading is free and stays free: feeds, People, Conversations, Videos, Email, the Morning Edition and everything else you read with. The Reading Room Plus is for writing to your own site. It costs $2.99 a year in the US (the App Store shows the price where you live), and the first week is free.

What comes with Plus

  • Post to your site: notes, posts, reposts, replies and likes, straight to your own WordPress site, with copies to Mastodon and Bluesky.
  • Writing: the notebook page, your ideas, the drafts on your site and the writing calendar, in one place.
  • Weeknotes: publish your Weeknotes and What I read this week as posts.
  • Your blog: visitors, RSS readers and search traffic for your blog.

Start or subscribe

Open Settings › The Reading Room Plus, or press Post in Writing. Apple’s own purchase sheet shows the price and the free week before you confirm. If your site was already connected when Plus arrived, posting stays free for three months; the app shows the date it ends.

Cancel, or check when it renews

Plus renews once a year until you cancel. On iPhone or iPad: Settings › your name › Subscriptions. On a Mac: App Store › your name › Account Settings › Subscriptions. Cancel at least a day before the free week ends and you won’t be charged. After you cancel, Plus keeps working until the end of the year you paid for, and everything you wrote stays on your site.

Restore on a new device

Plus follows your Apple Account. On another iPhone, iPad or Mac signed in to the same account, open Settings › The Reading Room Plus › Restore purchases. Family Sharing isn’t available for Plus.

Plus is in version 1.2.9 for iPhone, iPad and Mac, out now. Purchases are handled by Apple under Apple’s standard terms of use; see Privacy.

My Day

My Day is an experiment: a page to start the day on and check in with, beyond reading. Turn it off in Settings › Experimental if you don’t want it; the rest of the app doesn’t need it.

Coming up and the month

The top of My Day is four boxes, two by two when the window is wide enough; on a big screen, My Day spreads out to use it. Coming up lists your events for the next two weeks, day by day, each marked with its calendar’s color, and 🎂 for birthdays. The month shows your events at a glance; use ‹ › to change months, and tap a day to see it in Coming up (Back to today returns). Both read Calendar on the device, after it asks your OK once; your events never leave it.

Stocks and Crypto

Up to five stocks or funds and five coins, each as a tile: the price, the day’s change, where the price sits between the day’s low and high, and a trend line. Coins show the last 7 days and their market cap. Stocks show the open and the previous close, and their line builds up from the prices the app keeps on your device each day, so it appears after a few days. Stock prices need your own free key from finnhub.io: sign up there, copy your API key, and paste it in Markets in Settings. Coins need nothing. Tap a tile to open its page. Prices may be delayed, and none of this is investment advice.

My Day settings

My Day settings, at the top of My Day, has every option in one place: whether the app opens on My Day in the morning and until what time (9 am, 10 am, 11 am or noon), and links to Goals and rituals, Calendar, reminders and Day One, and Markets. When the app opens on My Day by itself, it says so, with a link to change it.

Morning ritual and goals

If you set a morning ritual, it shows on My Day for you to tick off. Set your rituals and season goals in Goals and rituals, from My Day settings. Your goals show in the Weekly Preview and at the end of your Day One page.

Put it down

Type a task, an event or a note for tonight’s journal. Write the time the way you’d say it (“tomorrow 9am”, “Tuesday 12:30”, “Nov 25 at 3pm for 2 hours”) and check the date shown underneath before you add it. Tasks go to Reminders, in a list called The Reading Room that’s made the first time; pick another list, or All my lists, in Calendar, reminders and Day One in My Day settings. Events go to your calendar. A pasted link goes to Read later.

To do

Your reminders, from The Reading Room list or the list you picked, read from Reminders on each device after it asks your OK once. Tick one to finish it. They never leave the device. On Today, a line under the greeting shows your next event and any birthday in the next five days, and My Day in the sidebar shows how many events are left today, with a 🎂 when a birthday is coming.

Markets on Today

Your stocks and coins also show in one row under the top story on Today. Turn that row off in Markets in Settings.

Day One

Turn on the Day One buttons in Calendar, reminders and Day One in My Day settings. Write yesterday’s page in Day One starts an entry with your own journal questions as headings, with what happened, what you read or heard and what’s next already filled in. Change the questions in the same place. Day One’s link can’t set the date, so change the entry’s date to yesterday in Day One. Any story’s Share menu has Journal in Day One too.

Evening and Weekly Preview

After 6 pm My Day turns to the evening: how today went, with your evening ritual and the notes you put down, and Tomorrow, your calendar for the next day. On Sundays the Weekly Preview looks back at the week, checks your goals, and asks what you read that mattered. It ends with a private page for Day One and Weeknotes for your site, which you can finish in Writing: they go in a Weeknotes category, with a featured image if you add one. Your goals and what got in the way never go in Weeknotes.

Email

Before social media, people with websites found each other by email. Connect one address and write to the people you read, from the app. It works best with an address on your own domain that you use just for the app.

  1. Open Settings › Experimental › Email (or the gear in the Email tab).
  2. Choose your provider: IONOS, Gmail, iCloud Mail, Fastmail, Yahoo, or Another provider with its mail server details.
  3. Type your name, address and password. Gmail, iCloud Mail, Fastmail and Yahoo need an app password, which you make in your account’s security settings.

Email then appears in the sidebar under Morning Edition, with your newest 100 emails. Use New email, Reply, or Write back › Reply by email on any post whose writer lists an address. Delete moves an email to your mailbox’s Trash. Each email ends with “Sent from The Reading Room”; change or remove it under Signature in Email settings.

People and Newsletters above the list keep emails written to you apart from newsletters, stores and services; Everything shows both. Replies to the same email show as one conversation, with a number beside the sender, and the rest of the conversation is listed at the end of each email. Show older emails, at the end of the list, brings in the 100 before the oldest one in the app. When you search, Search your whole inbox asks your mail server too and brings in the emails that match.

Pictures from the web wait until you tap Show pictures, because loading them tells the sender when you read their email. Choose Always for a sender to always show theirs, or change this under Pictures from the web in Email settings. Photos sent inside an email always show. Files attached to an email are listed at the end: Open or Save… on the Mac, Open… on iPhone and iPad. Each file is downloaded from your mailbox when you open it.

Under the sender you’ll find Reply all (when others got the email too), Forward, and Unsubscribe on newsletters, which asks first and then asks the newsletter to take your address off. If you follow the sender’s blog, it says so there, and the list shows their site’s icon. Archive moves an email out of the inbox into your mailbox’s Archive folder (press e on the Mac). On iPhone, swipe an email left to archive it, or right to mark it read or unread. After Archive or Delete you have a few seconds to press Undo. Mark all read is at the top.

Follow as a feed, under the sender of a newsletter, looks for the newsletter’s own feed. Many have one (Substack, Ghost and Beehiiv newsletters always do). Choose Follow and unsubscribe to read it with your other feeds and stop the emails, or Just follow.

When you write, Attach… adds files (up to 20 MB in all), and a forward can include the email’s own files: untick any you want to leave out. The To line suggests people you’ve emailed with. If you close an email before sending it, it’s kept as a draft on that device, marked Draft in the list, and comes back when you open it again. To get an alert when a person writes to you, turn it on when the Email tab offers it, or in Email settings; newsletters, stores and services never alert.

Folders. Inbox, Sent, Archive, Trash and Spam are listed under Email in the sidebar (tap the arrow to fold them). A folder is fetched from your mailbox when you open it. In Archive, Trash or Spam, Move to Inbox (or Not spam in Spam) puts an email back in your inbox; on iPhone, swipe it left. Empty Trash…, at the top of Trash, asks first and then deletes what’s there for good.

Each device connects its own mailbox, so you can use different ones on your Mac and your iPhone. Proton Mail works on a Mac through Proton Mail Bridge: choose Proton Mail and use the password Bridge shows under Mailbox details; the app works with Bridge set to SSL or STARTTLS. Connecting an account checks sending too, so a wrong setting shows right away. Outlook and Hotmail addresses aren’t supported, and school or work Google accounts often block app passwords.

More features

Help and feedback

Settings › Help and feedback has Send feedback (a GitHub form with your version filled in; a free GitHub account is needed and it’s public), Rate The Reading Room, and Help on the web.

Show in the sidebar and Experimental

Settings › Appearance › Show in the sidebar hides Videos or Books if you don’t use them. Settings › Experimental turns My Day, Email, Morning Edition and Help Wanted on or off; with all four off, The Reading Room is simply your feed reader. Each also has Hide from sidebar under its title. Nothing is deleted, and the choice reaches your other devices.

Trending in your neighborhood

On Today: names, topics and links that several of the people you read wrote about this week, more than usual. Tap one to see their posts side by side. It’s worked out on your device.

Quote cards and What I read this week

Select a passage and choose Quote card (or use it under a highlight) to make a picture of it to share. What I read this week, on Today and in Recently read, makes a short list of what you read, with a line about each. With your site connected, your picks go into Writing: into your Weeknotes, or as a post of their own. You can also send the list by email or copy it.

Settings and Appearance

Settings, at the bottom of the sidebar, holds Import and Export, Feed checkup, Muted words, Rules & alerts, Your year, your connections and sync. Appearance sets System, Light, Sepia or Dark for the whole app. How to use this app starts with a list of its sections.

Posts that need a subscription

A small amber lock means the site usually needs a subscription, or the post is for paying members. If you subscribe, open that feed and choose I subscribe to this site, and sign in once: on a Mac in the app’s web window, on iPhone or iPad in Safari. Free to read, next to All and Unread, hides locked posts.

Notes on your site

With your WordPress site connected, Share › Note on your site posts a short note about a story (for longer pieces, use Writing). It ends with a card showing the post’s picture, title and site, and that picture becomes the note’s featured image unless you add your own photo. Tick Then open X with a copy to open X’s own post window with your words and link; nothing is posted until you press Post there. A note with a title keeps it: the copies for Bluesky, Mastodon and X start with the title.

Move the note out of the way. Drag the note by its top bar to anywhere on the screen. The video or article behind it keeps working, so you can play, rewind and scroll while you write. Tuck away shrinks the note to just its top bar; Open brings it back. Double-click the bar to put the note back in the middle. If you close it before posting, your words are kept for next time.

The Morning Edition

Under Today in the sidebar. It lays out yesterday’s stories as a front page each morning at 6. Print and Mark the edition read are at the top. Stories with pictures get them, and In pictures collects a few more.

Newsletters

Read newsletters beside everything else instead of in your inbox. The app makes you a private email address; you sign up for newsletters with it, and each issue arrives as a story.

  1. Choose + Add a feed at the bottom of your feeds, then Read newsletters here.
  2. Under Name this address, type a name, like the newsletter’s name, and choose Make an address. A feed with that name joins your Newsletters folder (choose another under Put its feed in).
  3. Choose Copy address, go to the newsletter’s sign-up page, and paste it in place of your own email.
  4. Refresh after a minute. The newsletter’s welcome email arrives as a story. If it asks you to confirm, open it and tap the confirm link.

One address can take many newsletters, and they’ll share one feed. Make one per newsletter to keep them apart. Your addresses are listed under Read newsletters here whenever you need to copy one again. To stop a newsletter, use the unsubscribe link in any issue, then choose Remove next to its address.

The addresses come from Kill the Newsletter, a free, open-source service. Your own email address is never used. Anyone who knows an address can send to it, so use it only for newsletters.

Rules and alerts

Rules & alerts at the bottom of the sidebar. Choose words, a feed or any feed, and what to do: let you know, save to Read later, or mark read. Rules act on new stories as they arrive. Allow notifications when asked, or alerts can’t appear.

Watch a page

Choose + Add a feed, then Watch a page that has no feed, and paste the page’s address. Each refresh checks it, and any change arrives as a story. Pages that change on every visit, like ones with clocks or rotating ads, work less well.

Save from Safari

In Safari, open Share and choose Reading Room. The page is in Read later the next time you open the app. Don’t see it? On iPhone and iPad, scroll to the end of the row of apps, tap More and turn it on. On the Mac, choose Edit Extensions… at the bottom of the Share menu.

Widgets

Open the app once, then add Latest stories or Reading streak: on iPhone and iPad, touch and hold the Home Screen and tap Edit › Add Widget; on the Mac, right-click the desktop and choose Edit Widgets…. Widgets need iOS 17 or macOS Sonoma or later.

Your year, Feed checkup and Search

Your year and Feed checkup are at the bottom of the sidebar; both count the stories you open from the day they arrived. Type in the search box to see Older stories below your list, too.

Common questions

Some stories only show a summary.

A few sites keep their articles behind a paywall or build their pages with scripts the reader can’t use. Choose View original to read those on the site.

Where is my information stored?

Only on your devices, and in your own iCloud Drive if you turn on sync. See the privacy policy.

Which Mac do I need?

The Mac version needs macOS 13 Ventura or later, starting with version 1.2.9. iPhone and iPad need iOS or iPadOS 16 or later.

How do I move my feeds to another Mac?

Choose Feeds › Export OPML… on the old Mac and Import OPML… on the new one.

For site owners

Add a Follow button to your site

Give your readers a button that opens The Reading Room and offers to follow your site. Make your button: type your site’s address and copy the code into your site, or the link into your newsletter. Readers without the app are offered it free, along with your address for any other reader.

Show your blog’s stats in the app

On WordPress, the free plugin The Reading Room stats lets Writing show your visitors, RSS readers and search traffic. It counts on your site, without cookies or stored IP addresses.

Do feed:// links work?

Yes. A feed:// link opens in The Reading Room, which asks before following.

Contact

Questions, problems or ideas? Trying the beta, send feedback from TestFlight; a screenshot helps. Or open an issue on GitHub, where you can see what’s already been reported.
