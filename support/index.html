New to RSS?

Almost every blog and news site publishes a feed, a simple list of its newest posts. The Reading Room checks the feeds you choose and shows you what’s new, in order, with no algorithm in between. Inside the app, New to RSS? at the bottom of the sidebar (or Help › The Reading Room Guide on the Mac) walks you through it and suggests a few good people to follow.

Getting started

Add a feed

Press ⌘N, then paste a website address. The app finds its feed. Or type a few words, like pharmacy news, to follow that topic.

Make folders

Click the folder button next to Feeds, then drag feeds onto a folder. To put a folder inside another, like Blogroll › Charlotte NC, choose Inside when you make it, or drag one folder onto another; drag it onto All to bring it back to the top. Folders go two levels deep, and inner folders are listed first.

Coming from another reader?

Export your subscriptions as an OPML file there, then choose Feeds › Import OPML…. Folders come along.

Reading

Click a story to read it in the app. View original opens the full web page in the app’s own window.

Select text in an article and choose Highlight or Add note. Find them all under Annotated.

Conversations

Conversations turns the blogs you read into a social feed. It has three tabs.

Neighborhood

The posts of the people you read from the last two weeks, newest first, with their faces. No account needed. “Since you were here” at the top sums up what’s new; fold it to one line with –. When writers answer each other on their blogs, the replies gather under the original post as a thread. Faces, threads and links fill in as your feeds refresh, so give it a minute the first time. Following fewer than five people? It suggests a few to start with, and Writers your writers link to suggests blogs that the people you read keep linking to (× stops suggesting one).

A page for each person

Tap a face or a name, or choose Their page on a feed: their posts, who they talk with, emails from them, the blogs they link to and their blogroll.

Like and reply

Like without a site connected keeps the like just for you. With your WordPress site connected (Settings › Connections), the first like asks to make a Likes page on your site; after that each like is added there and the writer’s site is told, if it accepts Webmentions. Like again to take it back. Reply opens a box in the post: post your reply on your own site, or email the writer.

Following and You

Following shows what people share on Mastodon or Bluesky once you connect an account. You gathers replies to you there, comments on your own site, blogs linking to you and emails from people you read; the buttons above it show just one kind.

Email

Before social media, people with websites found each other by email. Connect one address and write to the people you read, from the app. It works best with an address on your own domain that you use just for the app.

  1. Open Settings › Connections › Email (or the gear in the Email tab).
  2. Choose your provider: IONOS, Gmail, iCloud Mail, Fastmail, Yahoo, or Another provider with its mail server details.
  3. Type your name, address and password. Gmail, iCloud Mail, Fastmail and Yahoo need an app password, which you make in your account’s security settings.

Email then appears in the sidebar under Morning Edition, with your newest 100 emails. Use New email, Reply, or Write back › Reply by email on any post whose writer lists an address. Delete moves an email to your mailbox’s Trash. Each email ends with “Sent from The Reading Room”; change or remove it under Signature in Email settings.

Each device connects its own mailbox, so you can use different ones on your Mac and your iPhone. Proton Mail works on a Mac through Proton Mail Bridge: choose Proton Mail and use the password Bridge shows under Mailbox details. Outlook and Hotmail addresses aren’t supported, and school or work Google accounts often block app passwords.

More features

Settings and Appearance

Settings, at the bottom of the sidebar, holds Import and Export, Feed checkup, Muted words, Rules & alerts, Your year, your connections and sync. Appearance sets System, Light, Sepia or Dark for the whole app. How to use this app starts with a list of its sections.

Posts that need a subscription

A small amber lock means the site usually needs a subscription, or the post is for paying members. If you subscribe, open that feed and choose I subscribe to this site, and sign in once: on a Mac in the app’s web window, on iPhone or iPad in Safari. Free to read, next to All and Unread, hides locked posts.

Notes on your site

With your WordPress site connected, Share › Note on your site posts a short note about a story. It ends with a card showing the post’s picture, title and site, and that picture becomes the note’s featured image unless you add your own photo. Tick Then open X with a copy to open X’s own post window with your words and link; nothing is posted until you press Post there.

The Morning Edition

Under Today in the sidebar. It lays out yesterday’s stories as a front page each morning at 6. Print and Mark the edition read are at the top. Stories with pictures get them, and In pictures collects a few more.

Newsletters

Read newsletters beside everything else instead of in your inbox. The app makes you a private email address; you sign up for newsletters with it, and each issue arrives as a story.

  1. Choose + Add a feed at the bottom of your feeds, then Read newsletters here.
  2. Under Name this address, type a name, like the newsletter’s name, and choose Make an address. A feed with that name joins your Newsletters folder (choose another under Put its feed in).
  3. Choose Copy address, go to the newsletter’s sign-up page, and paste it in place of your own email.
  4. Refresh after a minute. The newsletter’s welcome email arrives as a story. If it asks you to confirm, open it and tap the confirm link.

One address can take many newsletters, and they’ll share one feed. Make one per newsletter to keep them apart. Your addresses are listed under Read newsletters here whenever you need to copy one again. To stop a newsletter, use the unsubscribe link in any issue, then choose Remove next to its address.

The addresses come from Kill the Newsletter, a free, open-source service. Your own email address is never used. Anyone who knows an address can send to it, so use it only for newsletters.

Rules and alerts

Rules & alerts at the bottom of the sidebar. Choose words, a feed or any feed, and what to do: let you know, save to Read later, or mark read. Rules act on new stories as they arrive. Allow notifications when asked, or alerts can’t appear.

Watch a page

Choose + Add a feed, then Watch a page that has no feed, and paste the page’s address. Each refresh checks it, and any change arrives as a story. Pages that change on every visit, like ones with clocks or rotating ads, work less well.

Save from Safari

In Safari, open Share and choose Reading Room. The page is in Read later the next time you open the app. Don’t see it? On iPhone and iPad, scroll to the end of the row of apps, tap More and turn it on. On the Mac, choose Edit Extensions… at the bottom of the Share menu.

Widgets

Open the app once, then add Latest stories or Reading streak: on iPhone and iPad, touch and hold the Home Screen and tap Edit › Add Widget; on the Mac, right-click the desktop and choose Edit Widgets…. Widgets need iOS 17 or macOS Sonoma or later.

Your year, Feed checkup and Search

Your year and Feed checkup are at the bottom of the sidebar; both count the stories you open from the day they arrived. Type in the search box to see Older stories below your list, too.

Common questions

Some stories only show a summary.

A few sites keep their articles behind a paywall or build their pages with scripts the reader can’t use. Choose View original to read those on the site.

Where is my information stored?

Only on your devices, and in your own iCloud Drive if you turn on sync. See the privacy policy.

How do I move my feeds to another Mac?

Choose Feeds › Export OPML… on the old Mac and Import OPML… on the new one.

For site owners

Add a Follow button to your site

Give your readers a button that opens The Reading Room and offers to follow your site. Make your button: type your site’s address and copy the code into your site, or the link into your newsletter. Readers without the app are offered it free, along with your address for any other reader.

Do feed:// links work?

Yes. A feed:// link opens in The Reading Room, which asks before following.
